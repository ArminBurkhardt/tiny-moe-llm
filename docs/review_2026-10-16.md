# Research round after the pilot read, 2026-10-16

Written after both pilot arms were read (the "Run" section of [plans/PILOT.md](plans/PILOT.md)):
what the literature says about each of the pilot's open questions, and the directions it suggests.
Nothing here is decided; the decision table in PILOT.md and the decisions listed in NEXT.md "Now"
stay the user's. Papers are cited by arXiv number; the ones marked "new" were not in the plan's
references before this round.

## The leak (A1(b))

The nearest published relative is Limited Memory Language Models (LMLM, ICLR 2026, arXiv
2505.15962, new): from-scratch 124M to 382M models on about 3B Wikipedia tokens for 8 epochs, every
fact written inline as a lookup call whose returned value is masked from the loss, so the model
learns to look facts up rather than store them. With the database disabled the model still holds
some facts: FactScore 12.8 against 14.0 for a standard model, T-REx exact match 38.5 against 52.0;
the authors write that "some knowledge still remains unremoved". Co-LMLM (arXiv 2607.07707, new),
the continuous-query successor at 135M and 360M, reads the same way: TriviaQA 31.4 with the store
and 6.0 without, against 7.5 for a standard model. A 2026 audit of LMLM deletions (arXiv
2607.00605, new) keeps the model fixed and changes the database at inference (FULL, DEL-ON,
DEL-OFF): parametric leakage is near zero on its probes and the surviving residual (0.7% to 13.6%)
comes from near-neighbour retrieval. Every one of these judges the residual against the standard
model, which is the pilot's paired reading; none reports zero. The pilot's instrument sees a 0.011
residual at tier 100 because it is sharper than theirs, not because the recipe is worse.

Two levers from this line. Goldfish loss (arXiv 2406.10209, NeurIPS 2024, new) drops a
pseudo-random quarter of tokens from the next-token loss and reports large drops in extractable
memorization at little benchmark cost; read against the micro arms, where the masked arm (b) stored
nothing and the real-value arm (c) leaked, that suggests a mask-rate dose curve on the real-value
spans as the leak lever, in place of the swap rate (whose 0.30 branch moved nothing). Knowledgeless
Language Models (arXiv 2607.12831, new) anonymize entities in pretraining and report closed-book
recall down with contextual QA, fact verification and hallucination detection up by 20 to 25%
relative and better calibration and abstention; the placeholder arm (2026-10-08) was judged on
`delta` alone and read null, so the anonymization lever may pay on a grounded-task read instead.
"To Memorize or to Retrieve" (arXiv 2604.00715, new; OLMo-2 30M to 3B, up to 100B tokens) finds
retrieval's gains front-loaded, most of the benefit by one retrieval token per parameter, and
retrieval complementing rather than replacing parametric learning; the pilot sits at about 6.6
evidence tokens per parameter, past that region.

## Composition (L1)

Three findings line up with the pilot's chain read. First, in-context two-hop in a three-layer toy
transformer (arXiv 2502.13913, new) has a long slow phase of random guessing among the distractor
chains, in which the query attends uniformly over the bridge candidates and the model emits an
answer-type entity without selecting it, followed by an abrupt transition to near-perfect accuracy
(around step 800 of fresh data in their setup). That slow-phase mechanism describes the pilot's
chain read exactly: gold NLL at ln K, the answer type right, the choice wrong. Second, two 2026
from-scratch studies on parametric two-hop find that vanilla transformers never compose out of
distribution at any depth tested (8, 16 and 24 layers, 3.5M steps; arXiv 2608.07261 and
2604.07822, both new), while a looped block does: 0.84 to 0.97 OOD two-hop with one loop of two
shared four-layer halves; non-trivial from two recurrences, best with Poisson-sampled loop counts,
and inference-time loops extrapolating to deeper chains (R=8 trained to 16 hops reaches 24), with
over-thinking past about 15 iterations. Both needed grokking-scale training, around 10^4 epochs over
the fact set, and a three-stage timeline (memorize, in-distribution, systematic). Third, the
identity-bridge paper (arXiv 2509.24653, new) shows that one zero-hop self-mapping example per
bridge entity unlocks OOD two-hop even in a one-layer model, a cheap data addition. Ouro (arXiv
2510.25741; 1.4B and 2.6B, 7.7T tokens, 4 loops) confirms the plan's axiom at scale: capacity stays
at about 2 bits per parameter looped or not, and loops buy manipulation and multi-hop. The mechanism
papers on pretrained LLMs ("Hopping Too Late", arXiv 2406.12775: back-patching a later layer's state
into an earlier layer fixes up to 66% of two-hop failures; "Layer-Order Inversion", arXiv
2601.03542, new: the hops do not map onto successive depths) say the same thing from the other side:
re-feeding a later state to the same block is what a loop does. The pilot's 100M chain tokens in one
pass are nowhere near the grokking regime these papers needed, so the honest label for the pilot's
L1 is "under-dosed", and the architecture claim (looping is what composes) has independent support.

## The retriever (A4)

Co-LMLM is the fix. The query is the model's own L2-normalized last-layer hidden state at a special
token; the store's keys are the model's own states over the corpus (no separate encoder); the query
is trained with a bidirectional InfoNCE over in-batch negatives (temperature 0.07, weight 0.25 next
to the next-token loss), and the retrieved span is copied and masked from the loss. That is the
pilot's copy-first recipe plus the missing contrastive signal. Atlas (arXiv 2208.03299) and REPLUG
(arXiv 2301.12652) give the alternative where the retriever is distilled from the LM's own answer
likelihood. The pilot's selection BCE over a document's few visible chunks cannot produce a
retriever, which is what A4 measured (recall@5 0.004 against bge 0.829).

## Over-refusal (A3)

Over-abstention with sufficient context is a measured RAG failure across sizes (0.16 to 0.26 on
LIT-RAGBench, arXiv 2603.06198, new), prompting does not fix it (arXiv 2608.27661, new), and the
fixes for 2B to 7B models are RL with sufficiency-aware rewards (RSRR, SIGIR 2026; GRACE, arXiv
2601.04525; both new). That is 6f's three-way abstention reward, so deferring A3's behaviour to 6f
matches the field.

## Instrument note

Physics of Language Models 3.3 (arXiv 2404.05405) puts full capacity, about 2 bits per parameter,
at about 1,000 exposures. At pilot shape the control stores tier 100 and tier 1000 equally (0.467
and 0.468) and tier 10 at 0.20, so the top two tiers have lost resolution; the next biography build
wants tiers between 10 and 100.

## Directions (proposed, not decided)

1. A chain-overtraining arm from arm P's final save on the chain slice alone, with Poisson-sampled
   loop counts, read every 10 to 20M for the abrupt 2-hop transition (the toy paper's slow phase is
   set by the distractor count, and the chain documents carry 6 to 14 distractor chunks). The
   cheapest honest L1 read; it also tests inference-time loop extrapolation on 4-hop.
2. Zero-hop identity questions as a template flag in `prepare_chain_data.py`.
3. InfoNCE with in-batch store negatives on the model's own query, keys from the model's own
   encoder, as the A4 fix and possibly as the replacement for bge keys in the real run.
4. A mask-rate dose curve (goldfish style) on the real-value spans as the leak lever.
5. The FULL / DEL-ON / DEL-OFF deletion audit as the A7 read in place of the flip rate.
6. STARS-style Jacobian spectral-radius regularization (arXiv 2605.26733, new) if deeper or
   dynamic loops enter the spec, since several papers report loop performance peaking then
   collapsing.
7. Read the anonymization lever on grounded-task gains and calibration, not on `delta`.
