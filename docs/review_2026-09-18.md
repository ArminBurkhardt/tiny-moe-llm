# Codebase review before the Phase 4 spend (2026-09-18, revised the same day)

A read of the plan ([NEXT.md](plans/NEXT.md)), every measurement record under
[measurements/](measurements/), the model flow, the evidence port, the corpus builder, the
trainer, the evidence smoke logs and the smoke corpus's realized counts, cross-checked against
RETRO, InstructRetro, Fusion-in-Decoder, Memory Layers at Scale, Engram, LMLM and the 2026
looped-LM papers listed at the end. The first pass of this review was written before the smoke
corpus state was read; the revision below re-checks each finding against the code and the
realized numbers, marks what changed, and adds the findings the first pass missed. Every
reference was verified to exist on arXiv on 2026-09-18.

**Headline.** The Phase 4 run as built will very likely miss G3 and G3b for structural reasons,
not training reasons. Four of those reasons are fixable in a day each and two of them change the
on-disk format, so they belong before the full corpus build. Independently of Phase 4, the gates
cannot be *read* at all yet: no eval script in the repo can attach evidence. Below that, the
repo's conclusion that a parametric memory "cannot" learn facts from LM cross-entropy is
contradicted by the literature and should not drive the real run's spec. And for the project's
stated end goal (facts in the retrieval pathway, language and reasoning in the trunk, accurate or
abstaining outputs) the closest published precedent is LMLM, whose recipe is a *pretraining*
recipe, which means the real run has to be designed around retrieval from token 0 rather than
pretrained plain and grafted afterwards. That is the one deviation from the plan that matters
most, and it is argued in §2.

## 1. Before the Phase 4 spend (ordered by severity)

### 1.1 The reader attends over uncontextualized 32-d token embeddings — confirmed

`TinyMoETransformer.build_evidence` ([transformer.py](../modules/model/transformer.py)) builds
the evidence states with `_moe_ple`, i.e. the 65536 x 32 `moe_embeddings` table projected to 768
by one linear. No decoder layer ever touches the evidence, and the states do not change across
loops.

The 3.23-nat ceiling in [evidence_ceiling.md](measurements/evidence_ceiling.md) was measured with
the passage going through all 8 decoder layers plus 3 loops of the MoE block. A reader over a bag
of token identities with per-chunk RoPE can copy a token it already expects, but it cannot find
"the token after *born in*": no key carries any context, and the per-chunk position basis means
positions are not comparable to the query side either.

Every published side-channel design encodes the neighbours with a real encoder. RETRO runs a
bidirectional encoder over the retrieved neighbours before chunked cross-attention. Fusion-in-
Decoder encodes each passage independently (linear in passage count) and cross-attends in the
decoder. Nobody attends to raw token embeddings, for this reason.

**Fix.** Run the evidence chunks through the dense decoder once per forward (no loops, no LM head),
causal per chunk with the chunk's own `cu_seqlens`, and cache the result across the three loops.
Cost is roughly the decoder's 118 MFLOP per evidence token, i.e. about +70% forward at the QA
corpus's measured 2.93 evidence-to-prompt ratio, still linear in evidence with no quadratic term.
At the real run's ratio (§2.2) it is far less. A cheaper variant reuses only the first 2 to 4
decoder layers.

**De-risk first.** A 5M-token smoke A/B, PLE-embedded evidence against decoder-encoded evidence,
reading the gold-vs-none CE gap on the val split. If the PLE reader closes less than a third of
the ceiling, the design is the problem and no amount of the 150M-token run fixes it.

### 1.2 The seed is a pretrained trunk, not a chat model — confirmed

The smoke seeds from `checkpoint_ir_final_evidence.pt`, which descends from `phase2_final` via
the IR sharpening arm C. It has never seen the chat template: step 0 of the smoke log reads CE
6.57 on chat-formatted data. G3 and G3b are read through `eval_abstention.py`, i.e. through the
chat template, against numbers measured on the repair checkpoint (probe AUROC 0.584, precision
0.578). The run as planned would be learning the chat template, the abstention policy and the port
at once at lr 1e-5, and every delta would be confounded with the first two.

**Fix.** The table stores nothing (Stage 0), so the reshape is free. Migrate
`checkpoint_repair_final.pt` with `migrate_ir_reshape.py --arm random`, then
`migrate_evidence_port.py`, and seed Phase 4 from that.

### 1.3 Bug: the IR table is in the fresh-LR group, and nothing refreshes its clusters — confirmed, and worse than first written

`build_sft_param_groups` in [sft.py](../scripts/sft.py) computes
`is_fresh = is_rebuilt_ir_param(name) or is_fresh_loop_param(name)` whenever `fresh_lr` is set.
`is_rebuilt_ir_param` matches the whole `ir_module` subtree, so `z_keys`, `y_values`, `g_proj`,
`down_proj`, `up_proj` and `log_temperature` train at 3e-4 in the evidence profile. The smoke log's
"11 fresh/decayed, 3 fresh/undecayed" is exactly the port (6 + 2) plus the table (5 + 1): reader
q/k/v/o and the two adapters are the six decayed port tensors, the reader norm and
`log_memory_scale` the two undecayed ones, and `z_keys`/`y_values`/`g_proj`/`down_proj`/`up_proj`
plus `log_temperature` make up the rest. `EvidenceConfig`'s docstring in [config.py](../config.py)
and `migrate_evidence_port.py`'s report both claim the opposite.

The second half is new: only the `--ir` profile calls `refresh_ir_clusters` (the refresh is inside
`if args.ir:`). So the evidence profile trains the keys at the from-scratch rate for 150M tokens
with the centroids frozen at the seed's partition, and the two stage read's candidate recall,
which drifted from 0.995 to 0.917 across arm A's *refreshed* run, will silently degrade with
nothing measuring it. Whatever the parametric half of the selector's softmax is doing by the end
of the run, it will not be reading the table it started with.

**Fix.** A per-profile predicate: the evidence profile's fresh set is `is_fresh_loop_param` only.
Either that, or run the refresh in every profile that trains the keys.

### 1.4 Nothing trains the selector toward relevance, and the IR expert's output stage averages every read over the prefix — confirmed, with the mechanism

The IR expert is routed, and the aux loss pins its selection at 1/35 by construction. Its value
path then passes `g_proj` (stalled at RMS 0.0047, [ir_scale_fix.md](measurements/ir_scale_fix.md)),
`up_proj`, the expert's inner cross-attention, and the router gate. So `key_adapter`,
`value_adapter` and `log_memory_scale` receive gradient through ~6% of tokens times a ~200x
attenuated valve, and the mass split that G3b reads is trained by nothing direct.

The inner cross-attention deserves its own sentence, because it is the step that turns a read
into a bias term regardless of what the table stores. `InformationRetrievalExpert.forward`
([experts.py](../modules/model/experts.py)) computes `attn(hidden_states=x_norm,
other_states=information)`: queries from the residual, keys and values from each *position's own
retrieved vector*, causal over the document. Token `t` therefore does not receive its own read; it
receives a softmax-weighted mix of the reads of every position up to `t`. With `information` at
RMS 0.004 to 0.008 (arm C's pathway probe) and a default-init `k_proj`, the attention logits have
a standard deviation on the order of 1e-3, so the softmax is uniform to a fraction of a percent and
the expert emits `v_proj` of the running mean of the prefix's reads: a per-document constant. That
is not a hypothesis. Stage 0 measured that replacing the read by its batch mean costs **0.0000**
nats while zeroing it costs 0.0002, which is exactly what a prefix-averaging output stage
predicts, on every checkpoint, before and after the value normalization. The three arms could not
have moved this number: the scale fix quadrupled the input to a stage whose output does not depend
on which token retrieved what.

The corpus knows which chunk is gold; `apply_condition` shuffles it into the distractors and then
forgets which one it was.

**Fix, two parts.** First, store a per-chunk gold flag (one more `.evgold` uint8 file next to
`.evkey`, indexed by the same chunk axis) and add a selection loss on the per-chunk external mass
at the supervised positions (InfoNCE or BCE over the chunks visible to that document). It is
loadable-neutral, cheap, and trains the selector regardless of routing. This is a deviation from
"one objective through `pretrain.train_step` verbatim" and is worth it. Second, for anything
beyond Phase 4, take the inner attention out of the IR expert's output path and write the read to
the residual directly through a gate (§2.3); as long as the read is prefix-averaged before it
reaches the stream, no supervision on the selector can make the *content* of a retrieval matter.

### 1.5 G3b cannot discriminate SQuAD v2 with the signal as designed — confirmed

SQuAD v2's unanswerable questions were written adversarially against a *relevant* passage, so the
cosine between a query and bge(passage) is high in both classes. The external-mass split will
separate "evidence attached vs none", not "answerable vs not". The only component that can see
whether the answer span is present is the reader. The corpus agrees: a natively unanswerable
SQuAD row keeps its passage under the `gold` condition and still targets an abstention, and that
row is the only supervision in the corpus that separates "retrieved something relevant" from "can
answer".

**Fix.** Train a small groundedness head on the reader's output at the last prompt position with
the non-leaky label "gold chunk present and the row is answerable", which the corpus also knows.
This is the correctness-head pattern with an *external* label instead of the model's own argmax,
so it cannot collapse onto `p_max` the way `correct_proj` did. Keep G3b's 0.65 bar; read it on this
head and on the mass split separately.

### 1.6 Conversation weighting inverts the mix — confirmed, and the numbers are worse than estimated

The first pass estimated the split by conversation count at roughly QA 80%, web text 9%, chat
11%. The smoke corpus's own state file (`_prepare_state_evsmoke.json`) gives the realized counts:
14,393 QA rows, 1,033 web text rows, 978 replay rows, i.e. **QA 88%, web text 6%, replay 6% by
conversation** against QA 36%, web text 44%, replay 21% by token. Under `1 / n_supervised` the
20% replay floor is ~6% of the gradient, and the web-text arm, the only source that generalizes
the port past QA formatting, is ~6% too. A SQuAD answer of five supervised tokens gets a
per-token weight of 0.2; an 800-token web continuation gets 0.00125.

Part of the replay deficit is 1.11's shard-ordering problem, but the QA share is structural: a
QA conversation is ~50 prompt tokens and a web document is hundreds, so any corpus that mixes them
by token budget and then weights by conversation lands here.

**Fix.** Apply conversation weighting to the QA sources only, or cap the per-conversation weight
(e.g. `1 / sqrt(n)` or a floor of 1/64 per token), and print the *gradient* share per source at
build time next to the token share.

### 1.7 The loop-conditioned IR query is not built — confirmed

Stage 0 promoted it from a refinement to a precondition (`cos(q2, q3) = 0.99`). Phase 4 re-reads
the evidence at every loop with a query that does not move after loop 1, so the run tests reading,
not recursion over evidence, which is the stated objective. `loop_idx` reaches the IR module and
is used only to bucket the entropy instrumentation. It is a zero-init sinusoidal bias mirroring
`loop_router_bias`, a day of work, and neutral to load. Also note `last_memory_mass` is
overwritten every loop, so the G3b readout is the final loop's only.

Two things the first pass did not say. The reader has the same problem: `shared_evidence` reads
`step_input` as its query at every loop, so its three reads are near-copies of each other. And the
migrated `loop_scale` of `[0.63, 0.32, 0.11]` scales the *whole* accumulator, evidence read
included, so loop 3's re-read reaches the residual at a sixth of loop 1's gain by construction.
"Re-read at every loop" is, in practice, "read at loop 1".

### 1.8 Per-condition validation is missing, and it is the early-kill number — confirmed

The val pass reports one CE. Gold-vs-none CE gap on the val split *is* G3, and reading it at 10M
tokens is the kill criterion. Store the condition label per document (a `.cond` uint8 file) now,
before the full build, and report val CE per condition. Also log `|shared_evidence.o_proj|rms`
against its zero init (the same reason `|g_proj|rms` is logged) and mean external mass per
condition. The smoke's val split is 161 conversations in 2 batches, which is noise; the full
build's 1% is fine, but the per-condition split needs the label to exist.

### 1.9 Buffers never exceed ~6 chunks, and the length cap drops a third of the web text rows — reworked

The first pass claimed the 1,536-token evidence cap "will drop many HotpotQA `mixed` rows". The
smoke state says otherwise: 3 of 5,484 HotpotQA rows were dropped as too long. The reason is that
`mixed` is not "10 paragraphs": `num_distractors` defaults to 3, so a HotpotQA `mixed` row is 2 gold
plus 3 near distractors, ~600 tokens, and the cap does not bind. That part of the finding was
wrong.

What the state does show is the *web text* arm: 695 of ~2,100 rows dropped as too long against
1,033 kept. `split_webtext_document` holds out `max(128, 25%)` of the document and keeps the other
75% as prompt, so the 4,094-token prompt cap drops every document past ~5.4k tokens and the
1,536-token evidence cap every document past ~6.1k. That is the long-document tail, a third of the
web rows by count and more by token, and it is exactly the text where a held-out span carries the
most content the continuation depends on.

The valid core stands: the reader never sees more than about six chunks, while G6 asks for 64+ and
G5's needle eval for 256. The reader will be tested at a buffer size it has never been trained on.

**Fix.** Raise the web-text caps (or cut the held-out span to a fixed 256 to 512 tokens rather than
25% of the document) so the long tail survives; add a many-distractor condition (16 to 32 chunks)
on a subset of QA rows; and check the `too_long` counts per source after the build, which is a
number the builder already prints.

### 1.10 None of the eval scripts can attach evidence — new

`eval_abstention.py`, `eval_benchmarks.py`, `eval_calibration.py`, `eval_stage0.py` and
`inference.py` contain no reference to evidence, chunk keys or the memory mass. NEXT.md's "eval
flip" (`eval_abstention.py --evidence-port`, the passage leaving the prompt and entering the port)
is described as something the phase "gains" and is not built. Concretely:

- G3 (gold-vs-none CE gap on the answer span, through the port) cannot be read.
- G3b (AUROC of the external mass for unanswerable detection) cannot be read: nothing extracts
  `last_memory_mass` at the last prompt position of a batched left-padded decode.
- G5 and G6 need an index-backed inference path that does not exist, which is expected for Phase 5,
  but the batched decode in `eval_abstention.py` also has no evidence path, so even oracle-evidence
  EM cannot be measured.
- `CrossAttention.forward` passes `kv_cache=None` for evidence, so a cached decode would recompute
  the evidence K/V at every generated token; `inference.py` would need an evidence slot per loop.

The smoke run's only readable number is the val CE, which is what §1.8 says is not enough. Build
the evidence path into `eval_abstention.py` (teacher-forced and generated, per condition) before
the run, or the run produces a checkpoint nobody can score.

### 1.11 The replay share is the wrong data, and the holdout is not honoured — new

`prepare_evidence_data.py` lists a source's shards with `sorted(...)` and consumes them in that
order. For smoltalk2 the first shard in sorted order is
`SFT/LongAlign_64k_context_lang_annotated_lang_6_no_think`, the 64k-context split, which is what
the smoke's scratch cache shows it downloaded. The state file records 6,249 of 6,682 smoltalk2 rows
skipped as too long and 433 kept, so 93% of the replay source was dropped and the kept 7% are the
*shortest conversations of a long-context split*, not general chat. At 12% of a 150M-token build
the source would still be inside its first two or three shards. `prepare_sft_data.py` has the same
`sorted()` but never hit this because its holdout check removed those rows first (the repair state
shows smoltalk2 at file 9 with 1,032,758 holdout skips and zero too-long).

That is the second half: the evidence builder has no holdout check at all. `render_messages_row`
never consults `manifest.json`'s `smoltalk2_holdout_hashes`, so the replay slice reuses
conversations the phase-2 pretraining already consumed. CLAUDE.md states that the SFT builder
honours the holdout and refuses to run without it; the evidence builder silently does neither.

**Fix.** Shuffle the shard order per source with the seeded RNG the builder already has, or filter
the LongAlign split out of the replay sources; import the holdout check from `prepare_sft_data.py`
the same way the builder already imports `SQUAD_INSTRUCTION`.

### 1.12 The selector and the reader are not connected — new

NEXT.md's Option B says "seed the accumulator with the evidence read ... and gate the *content* by
retrieval scores instead". The reader is seeded into the accumulator; the gating is not
implemented. `shared_evidence` attends over every evidence token of the document with its own
softmax, and the selector's per-chunk mass is never used by it. The two halves of the port score
the same chunks independently: the selector's split is the quantity G3b reads, and the reader is
the quantity that decides what the model writes, and nothing ties them together. A selector that
learns relevance perfectly would change no output; a reader that learns to ignore distractors would
leave the mass split at whatever the adapters drifted to.

**Fix.** Gate the reader's evidence states per chunk by a function of the selector's chunk score
(segment-level, e.g. mean over the document's tokens of the chunk's external mass, mapped through a
learned scalar and a sigmoid), applied to the states before `k_proj`/`v_proj`. Flash varlen has no
additive bias, so a multiplicative gate on the states is the cheap coupling. It also gives the
selector a dense, always-on gradient through the reader, which solves the starvation in §1.4
without depending on the router. Combine with the supervised selection loss, not instead of it.

### 1.13 Smaller items

- `SQUAD_INSTRUCTION` says "the passage below" while nothing is below in evidence mode. Either
  extend the instruction consistently in the builder and the eval, or accept the mismatch.
- The `{phase} tokens` log field counts prompt tokens, not evidence tokens; the ETA is built on it.
- `.evidence_ceiling_probe.py` is referenced by a tracked doc but is untracked; move it under
  `scripts/`.
- The smoke log's per-loop CE has loop 3 worse than loop 2 at several log steps (step 0: 6.61 /
  6.41 / 6.57, step 70: 2.78 / 2.71 / 3.11). Out of distribution the last loop hurts; worth
  reading per condition once §1.8 exists.
- The smoke's step-0 aux loss was 3.06 in the first (killed) run and 1.22 in the second, against a
  balanced value of ~1.0. Nothing in the port touches routing; the difference is the packing fix.
  A routing statistic that moves 3x with row fill is a reminder that the aux loss is read over pads.

## 2. The real run and the architecture

The project's end goal is stated as: facts live in the retrieval pathway, language and reasoning
live in the rest of the network, and the model's outputs are accurate because it answers from
evidence or abstains. Two things about that goal are worth saying plainly before the design.

*"Perfectly accurate" is a property of a system, not of a weight file.* The mechanism that can
deliver it is "copy from retrieved evidence, or abstain", plus a check that the emitted answer is
supported by the retrieved chunks (an extractive-span constraint or a small entailment check at
inference). No architecture makes a 500M-parameter decoder incapable of emitting an unsupported
token; the architecture makes it *unnecessary*, and the verification step makes it *detectable*.
The run spec should name both halves.

*Separation is a pretraining property.* LMLM ([Zhao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15962))
is the closest precedent to this goal, and its recipe is: annotate the corpus with lookups, mask
the retrieved values out of the loss, and pretrain with the lookups present, so the trunk never
has a reason to memorize them. Its 382M model matches or beats LLaMA2-7B on FactScore (31.9 vs
34.0) and PopQA (50.8 vs 29.2), and disabling the lookup at inference drops FactScore 31.9 → 12.8,
which is the number that proves the facts live outside the trunk. This repo's 16B-token trunk was
trained with no lookup and full loss on every fact, so it stored what it could, and every later
phase is trying to talk it out of using that. The measured result is consistent: three finetunes
did not move the answerability representation ([answerability_probe.md](measurements/answerability_probe.md)),
and three IR arms did not move the read. The graft trajectory is 0 for 4 on gates
(G1, G2, G2b, G2c). A next run that pretrains plain and grafts the port afterwards inherits all of
this. The real run's pretraining itself has to carry evidence.

### 2.1 "A parametric table cannot learn facts from LM CE" is contradicted by Memory Layers at Scale — confirmed, venue corrected

Berges et al. (ICML 2025, arXiv 2412.09764; the first pass said ICLR) train a shared product-key
memory replacing 3 FFN layers from scratch at 134M to 8B parameters. At 1.3B and 1M keys NQ goes
7.76 → 13.68 and TriviaQA F1 32.6 → 42.9 against the dense baseline; at 64M keys NQ reaches 20.8
and TriviaQA 62.1, approaching Llama2-7B trained on 2T tokens at 10x the FLOPs. Gains are largest
at small base size and early in training, i.e. exactly this project's regime. Engram
([Cheng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372), DeepSeek) reaches the same conclusion with
hashed n-gram embeddings and adds an allocation result: at a fixed inactive-parameter budget, the
best split puts about 20 to 25% of it into the memory rather than into MoE experts, stable across
scales. The differences to this repo's IR expert are the whole story:

| | Memory+ (Berges et al.) | this repo's IR expert |
|---|---|---|
| value dim | hidden size | 384, then `g_proj`, then `up_proj` |
| output path | silu-gated direct residual add | inner cross-attention over per-token reads (a prefix average, §1.4), then the router gate |
| selection | exact top-k over 1M to 64M product keys | 256 centroids, 8 probed, refresh, recall warnings |
| routing | always-on | top-2 of 35, aux loss pins it at 1/35 |
| training | from scratch, up to 1T tokens | grafted onto a converged trunk, 208M tokens |

The read here passes four attenuators in series and one averaging stage. Three grafted arms
agreeing to four decimals is evidence about the graft and the pathway, not about whether LM CE can
teach a memory. If a parametric store is kept in the real run, build it as a Memory+ style layer:
from scratch, in the residual path, always-on, value dim equal to hidden, product-key exact top-k,
reused at every loop with the loop-conditioned query, sized at Engram's 20 to 25% of the sparse
budget. Do not freeze it out of the spec on the strength of Phase 3.

Whether to keep it at all is a different question, and the end goal answers it: a parametric store
is neither auditable nor editable, so it cannot carry the accuracy claim. The external store can.
The recommendation is therefore: the external evidence pathway is the mechanism the run is built
around and the claim is made about; the parametric memory is a compute-cheap cache that has to
earn its place in the from-scratch A/B, measured by the lookup-off ablation, not by CE.

### 2.2 Retrieval-augmented pretraining from token 0 — new, and the main deviation from the plan

NEXT.md's Phase 7 describes a plain pretraining run (7b: a ≥100B-token corpus from the same
seven-source mix) followed by the same post-hoc stack. Under §2's argument that ordering is
backwards. The published route is RETRO (frozen retriever, 64-token chunks, chunked
cross-attention, 25x fewer parameters at matched Pile perplexity) and InstructRetro (retrieval
continued-pretraining of a 43B model on 100B tokens for 2.6% extra GPU-hours, +7% on closed-book
QA), and RPT shows the retriever should be trained with the LM rather than frozen (Books3
perplexity 10.96 vs RETRO's 14.60 on the same setup).

What is affordable at ≤5k H100-hours, in order of cost:

1. **The same-document held-out span, for free.** `prepare_evidence_data.py`'s web-text
   construction (hold a span out of the document and attach it through the port) needs no index
   and scales to the whole corpus. Apply it to a fraction of pretraining documents (30 to 50%),
   with the held-out span at a fixed 256 to 512 tokens rather than 25% of the document, and attach
   1 to 3 random other documents' spans as distractors. This is the RETRO regime without the
   index, and it trains the reader and the "irrelevant evidence costs nothing" behaviour at
   pretraining scale.
2. **A real index for the QA-shaped and Wikipedia slices.** KILT's 21M passages embed with
   bge-small in a few hours on one GPU; one query per document (the first 256 tokens) against an
   IVF-PQ index is ~100M queries for a 100B-token corpus, feasible on CPU in a day. Attach the top
   4 neighbours, excluding the document itself.
3. **An LMLM-style masked slice.** On the Wikipedia and QA portions, mask the answer or fact span
   out of the loss when it appears verbatim in an attached chunk, so the trunk is never rewarded
   for storing it. This is what makes the lookup-off ablation a real measurement rather than a
   hope, and it is the cheapest possible version of LMLM's annotation.

The reader has to be part of the trunk from step 0 (Nanbeige's from-scratch-beats-upcycling
result applies to the port as much as to the loop), which means the encoder question in §1.1 is
answered by construction: evidence is encoded by the same dense decoder, once, with its own
`cu_seqlens`.

### 2.3 Too much attention per loop, and the doubtful modules are the expensive ones — confirmed

Each loop runs four attention modules (shared attention, self-attention expert, cross-attention
over the PLE embeddings, the IR expert's inner attention), five with the reader. Attention is 126
of 499 MFLOP forward. The cross-attention over token-identity embeddings and the IR inner
attention (attention over what *other* tokens retrieved, §1.4) have no published analogue and no
measured value. Ablate both in the from-scratch A/B, and let the IR expert write to the residual
directly.

### 2.4 The shape is inverted relative to every published looped design — confirmed

Eight dense prelude layers feed one looped block: the recurrent core is ~140 of ~484 MFLOP per
token (the decoder is 118, the heads 100, attention 126). Geiping, Mixture-of-Recursions and
SMELT all put most of the compute in the recurrent stack with a short prelude and a coda. The 7c
A/B should compare "4 prelude + a 2-block looped core x 3 + 2 coda" at matched compute, not only
"coda vs none".

### 2.5 Per-loop CE may be manufacturing the loop-3 redundancy — new hypothesis, cheap to test

`loop_ce_weights = [0.2, 0.3, 1.0]` plus `loop_count_sampling = 0.3` (which rescales the deepest
loop run to weight 1.0) means loop 1 is trained as a full-weight readout on 15% of steps and loop 2
on another 15%, and both carry substantial weight on every other step. The objective asks the
model to be a good 1-loop model, a good 2-loop model and a good 3-loop model at once. The marginal
value of loop 3 is then small *by construction*: whatever loop 2 must already read out well, loop 3
cannot add. This is consistent with everything measured (loop 2 ≈ loop 3 CE at the end of
pretraining, `cos(Δ3, Δ2) = 0.63 to 0.73`, arm D making loops more alike) and it is a training
cause rather than an architecture cause, which the plan has so far not considered. Geiping
supervises only the sampled final depth; Ouro uses an entropy-regularized exit rather than dense
readout supervision; and "Dense Supervision Is Not Enough" (§2.6) shows per-loop CE has its own
failure modes.

**Test.** Two from-scratch runs at reduced width, ~1B tokens each, local: the current weights and
sampling against final-depth-only supervision with a 10% sampled-depth fraction and a coda. Read
loop 3's gain and the depth curve at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6. This folds into 7c's LR sweep runs.

### 2.6 The readout blind spot — confirmed, reference verified

"Dense Supervision Is Not Enough: The Readout Blind Spot in Looped Language Models"
(Sharma and Vu, arXiv 2606.24898, June 2026) shows that RMSNorm readouts hide the state norm from
per-loop CE while the recurrence amplifies it, with 44M and 129M looped models driving final
hidden-state norms into the thousands, and that supervision alone cannot control recurrent scale.
Here `‖Δh‖/‖h‖` falls 0.87 / 0.36 / 0.10 across loops and `loop_scale` collapsed under the halt
gate. Log `‖h‖` per loop, and for any depth curriculum past 3 either normalize the state at loop
entry or make the scale visible to the loss.

### 2.7 Retrieval at every loop is the depth mechanism, and it needs multi-hop data — new

The literature scan found no published system that combines a trained recurrent-depth model with
a retrieval or memory read at every loop. That is the project's differentiated claim, and it is
also the only reason the loops have to exist: Virtual Logic Depth says loops buy computation, not
storage, so the loop's job here is to *re-query* with what the previous loop read. Three things
follow for the run spec:

- The candidate set is fetched once per sequence (ANN, k = 32 to 64), and each loop *selects*
  within it with a loop-conditioned query; the append-only buffer is the union of selections.
- The training data has to contain questions whose second hop is only retrievable after reading
  the first (HotpotQA's bridge questions, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue), with the hop-2 chunk in the
  candidate set. Without that, per-loop retrieval has nothing to learn and the depth curve will be
  flat for a data reason.
- The depth gate (G5) is read on those sets, with the selector's per-loop hit rate reported next to
  EM, so a flat curve is attributable to selection or to reading.

### 2.8 Throughput — reworked

The first pass wrote that MFU 11% is mostly `hidden_size` 768 and that FP8 "will buy little".
The second claim is unsupported. The local 5090 measured 31.8% MFU on the same shape in BF16, and
the H100's 11% of 990 TFLOPS is ~109 TFLOPS achieved against the 5090's ~66, so the H100 was
running the *same kernels* at half the utilization: the step is overhead-bound (35-expert routing
with three host syncs per loop through `m_splits`, four attention passes per loop, a chunked and
checkpointed head at 4x, no `torch.compile`, no CUDA graphs), not GEMM-width-bound. At `M = 32k`
tokens per micro batch a 768-wide GEMM is large enough for FP8 to help; what FP8 cannot fix is
launch and sync overhead. So 7a's order should be: profile the step first, `torch.compile` the
dense decoder and shared MLP, remove the per-loop host syncs, a bigger micro-batch on the 80GB
card, then the FP8 A/B. The heads are 100 of 499 MFLOP forward at 4x cost (fwd, recompute, bwd),
so tying the head plus a fused linear-CE plus subsampling the MTP heads (7d) is the largest single
win and also removes 13M parameters of rank-192 head. Run the oracle-head reading before the spec
is written.

### 2.9 Router specialization — reworked

The aux loss sat at its balanced value from step 0 with 32 experts at top-2, so each MLP expert saw
~1B tokens. "Mean routed weight is flat across all 35" is however an artefact the plan already
suspects: the top-2 scores are renormalized to sum to 1, so two near-equal logits give ~0.5 each
whatever the router has learned, and the flat mean says nothing. The selection *fractions* did
spread (0.03 to 0.24), which is the real signal, and it says the router did specialize somewhat.
Measure `aux_loss_weight` sensitivity on the existing checkpoint before the shape is set, and for
the real run prefer fewer, larger experts or a bias-based balancing that does not push the
non-MLP slots toward 1/35.

### 2.10 What the acceptance criteria for "separation" should be — new

CE on a held-out slice cannot see any of this. The real run's gates for the end goal are:

- **Lookup-off ablation**: closed-book factual EM and FactScore-style precision with the evidence
  pathway disabled, against enabled. LMLM's 19% relative drop is the floor to beat; a run whose
  factual accuracy barely moves when retrieval is switched off has stored the facts in the trunk.
- **Grounded precision**: fraction of emitted answer spans that appear in the attached evidence,
  under gold, mixed and distractor conditions separately.
- **Abstention curve**: precision ≥ 0.65 at recall ≥ 0.5 (G7's bar), measured with the passage in
  the port, not the prompt.
- **Distractor cost**: CE and EM under distractors-only must not be worse than under no evidence
  (the 0.63-nat penalty in [evidence_ceiling.md](measurements/evidence_ceiling.md) is the number to
  drive to zero).
- **Editability**: change a fact in the store and confirm the answer changes (LMLM's unlearning
  result is the template). This is the one test that a parametric-only design cannot pass.

repair lineage (1), the decoder encodes evidence (2), the three objective deviations are accepted
(3); question 4 is decided in the 7c A/B by the lookup-off ablation.

**One latent bug fell out of 1.12.** `attention._segment_ids` assigned `seg_id[cu_seqlens[1:-1]] = 1`, which a zero-length segment breaks two ways: a repeated boundary collapses into one increment (mis-numbering every later token) and a boundary at `B*S` is an out-of-range index, i.e. a device-side assert that surfaces as the process wedging with the GPU idle. Zero-length segments are legal and routine on the evidence axis — a document that retrieved nothing — so the function now accumulates into an oversized buffer and slices. The query side has neither case, so its ids are unchanged.

**The gold-vs-none CE gap needs a fixed target, which the first eval build did not have.** The
forced target legitimately changes with the condition — `none` and `distractors` force an abstention
— so a gap built on it compares "say the answer" against "say a refusal", two differently priced
targets, and the refusal wins whatever the port does (it read −3.25 nats, the wrong sign, on a seed
whose reader is provably zero). `--evidence-port` now runs a second teacher-forced pass with the
**real answer as the target under every condition**, over the natively answerable rows, and the gap
is read there. On the migrated seed it is exactly 0.0000 nats, which is the neutrality guarantee
showing up as a measurement; on the smoke-trained port it is −0.0136, i.e. the port still reads
essentially nothing — the same verdict the IR arms reached, now readable on the evidence pathway.

**`--evidence` and the KV cache are not a hard conflict**, unlike the convergence exit: with a live
reader the cached and uncached paths agree for several tokens and then a greedy argmax can flip,
because the reader's cross-attention runs at a different shape per step under caching and the
kernels need not tile identically — the same class of non-determinism `ParallelSparseMoELayer`'s
`m_splits` already has. Documented rather than blocked.

**Two flags are inferred from the state dict, not from the yaml**: `evidence_port` already was, and `ir_direct_read` now is, because a checkpoint's values were trained against one output stage and reading them back through the other scores a model nobody trained. `utils.NEUTRAL_LOOP_KEYS` covers the four genuinely neutral additions.

## 3. Suggested order, and three questions

**Before the corpus build** (format changes): 1.3, 1.6, 1.8, 1.9, 1.11, and the gold flag from 1.4.

**Before the run:** 1.10 (the evals, or the run cannot be scored), 1.1 (the 5M-token A/B decides
it), 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.7, 1.12.

**During the run:** kill at 10M tokens if the gold-vs-none val gap is under ~0.1 nats.

**Before the real run's spec (7c):** §2.5's from-scratch depth-supervision A/B alongside the LR
sweep; the oracle-head reading (7d); the retrieval-augmented corpus prep (§2.2) budgeted as its
own job; the gate table of §2.10 written into RUN2.md.

Questions whose answer changes the work:

1. Seed Phase 4 from the repair checkpoint (recommended) or keep the arm C lineage for continuity
   with the IR arms?
2. Is encoding evidence through the dense decoder acceptable? It breaks "evidence tokens are cheap"
   but keeps the cost linear, and the port is unlikely to reach the ceiling without it. For the
   real run it is answered by §2.2 regardless.
3. Are a supervised selection loss, a groundedness head and the reader gate acceptable deviations
   from "one objective through `train_step`"? All three use labels the corpus already knows, not
   the model's own outputs.
4. Does the real run keep a parametric memory at all (as a Memory+ layer, §2.1), or does the
   external store carry the mechanism alone? The accuracy claim only rests on the external store
   either way.
